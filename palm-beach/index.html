Proposal for sale by auction
Prepared 2 September 2026

24D  ·  973 Gold Coast Highway  ·  Palm Beach

Finding buyers was never the problem.

Three buyers have been at this table. Two signed contracts, at $1,850,000 and $1,800,000, and both were lost to finance. Seven days for sale, then a three-week auction — because an auction contract has no finance clause to fail.

The record

Three buyers. None of them settled.

This is the campaign’s own record rather than my opinion of it, and it points somewhere very specific.

$1,900,000+ An offer before the February auction, which was not accepted at the time. Not proceeded with
$1,850,000 A signed contract. The buyer could not complete. Lost — finance
$1,800,000 A second signed contract. Again the buyer could not complete. Lost — finance
$1,750,000 Where the asking price stands today. No buyer

Two things follow from that list. The first is that this apartment sells. Buyers have been found, terms have been agreed, and contracts have been signed — twice. That is the genuinely hard part of this job, and it has been done. The second is that it keeps failing in the same place. Not at the open home. At the finance clause.

The two campaigns also used different methods: the first took it to auction in February, the second has run private treaty since March. Each approach has been tried once, on its own. Neither has been tried in sequence, and neither has been paired with a contract a buyer cannot walk away from.

The market

This is not the market you listed into

24D came to market on 16 January, into conditions that were still rising. Those conditions peaked in May and have been unwinding ever since. That changes what a campaign has to do.

Cotality’s September index records the downturn broadening sharply: the share of capital city suburbs losing value more than doubled through winter, and national values now sit 3.6% below their March peak. Its research director notes that higher-value housing continues to record the larger declines, under elevated borrowing costs and serviceability limits.

That last point matters here more than anywhere. 24D sits at roughly double the Regional Queensland unit median of $827,133. It is firmly in the segment the index says is falling fastest — and the same serviceability pressure driving those falls is what took both of the contracts on this apartment. This apartment did not sell in the strongest conditions of the cycle. Time is no longer neutral; every month of delay now has a cost attached to it.

04

The strategy

Seven days for sale. Then a date.

Week one carries a price, to reach the buyers who search by budget. Then we set a date — and the campaign ends in a contract that has nothing left in it to fail.

For sale7 days
Auction campaign24 days · three weekends and auction day
This campaign — 7 days for sale, then a dateThe campaign as it has run

A campaign with a price gets one peak. Interest climbs through the first week, tops out, and falls away for the rest of the run — which is exactly the pattern the last six months have followed. This campaign gets two. The first is the launch. The second is the auction announcement on day eight, which starts the climb again and keeps it climbing, because from that moment the deadline is getting closer rather than further away. The highest point of the whole campaign is auction morning, when every remaining buyer is in the same room at the same time.

Why the tenth of October

Swipe the chart →

October is the busiest sales month of the year in Palm Beach. On Pricefinder’s figures it records a median of 45 sales, ahead of November at 43 and July at 41 — against a low of 26 in June. The campaign launches into September and calls the auction in the single strongest month on the calendar, while the market still has buyers in it. From there the year falls away hard: December drops to 28.

The obvious objection

“It went to auction in February and it didn’t sell.”

True, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a slogan.

  • The problem this solves is finance, not method.Two contracts have been signed and lost since then, both at the same clause. On the fall of the hammer a contract is unconditional: no finance clause, non-refundable deposit, settlement on your dates. The specific failure that has cost this campaign two sales cannot occur.
  • February was one method, on its own.Auction was tried, then private treaty was tried. This is neither in isolation — it is a week of price-led exposure to build a buyer list, then a deadline applied to that list.
  • A reserve is not agreed today.It is set with you in week three, from what the campaign actually produces: contracts issued, registered bidders, and the range buyers are talking in. I would rather set it late and set it right.

And if it passes in on the day. You are not starting again. I carry the campaign a further thirty days at no additional marketing cost, negotiating from the highest bid on the day with that bidder’s exclusive first right, and every buyer the four weeks qualified is in hand with their finance already checked. What we do on price from there is your decision, taken on what the campaign actually produced rather than on a number either of us guessed in September.

Phase oneThu 10 – Wed 16 September

A week with a price, to find who is actually out there.

  • It reaches the buyer who searches by budget.$1,750,000 has only been on the board since late July, and for part of that stretch the listing carried no price at all. A large share of buyers filter by price bracket — a good number of them have never seen this apartment at its current number.
  • Every enquiry is qualified on the spot.Price position, finance status, timing. That list is what phase two is aimed at, and it is the reason the auction has something to work with.
  • A strong early offer is still a real outcome.If one arrives unconditional in week one, it comes to you. Nothing about phase two is compulsory.
The switchDay 8 · Wed 16 September

The date goes on. That is the second news event.

  • Every week-one enquiry gets a call, not an email.“The owner has set an auction date” turns interest into a diary entry.
  • The listing genuinely changes.A change of method re-enters buyer alerts and puts the apartment back in front of people who had already moved past a price.
  • Two peaks of attention rather than one.A straight four-week auction spends everything at launch and then decays.
Phase two16 September – 10 October

Three weeks of auction, run properly.

  • Three opens a week, not one.Saturday, a midweek twilight and a Sunday. Across the campaign that is around thirteen inspection windows against roughly four a month today — more than three times the access, and the twilight opens exist because the view at dusk is the product.
  • Finance verified before a contract is issued.Written confirmation of approval or funds, deposit ready, solicitor engaged. Two contracts have been lost at exactly this point and I will not present a third on trust.
  • A written report every Monday.Enquiries, inspections, contracts out, the range buyers are talking in, and a reserve position that moves with the evidence.
Auction daySat 10 October · 11:00am · on site

On the balcony, not in a boardroom.

  • Competition sets the price.It is the only method where the ceiling is discovered rather than declared.
  • It sells unconditional.Non-refundable deposit, no finance clause, your settlement dates — which, given the last five months, is the whole point.

Never give up

Two methods have been tried separately. The third campaign should at least ask a different question.

I came to real estate from engineering and construction project management. I run a campaign the way I ran a build: sequenced, measured, and reported every week, with the failure points identified before they arrive rather than after. On this property the failure point is not a secret. It has happened twice, at the same clause, and the method I am proposing is the one that removes it.

I am originally from New Caledonia and I work in French and English. For a beachfront sub-penthouse that matters more than it sounds: a meaningful share of buyers at this level are offshore, and most agents cannot speak to them.

NGU stands for Never Give Up. It is the name the agency was built on, and a property two campaigns could not move is precisely the brief we are built for.
